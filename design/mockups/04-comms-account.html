What changes

  1. 1
    ADD A contract badge on every conversation row that has one. The inbox and the contract rooms currently share a conversation but not its context, so a member scanning the inbox cannot tell an idle "is this still available?" from the thread attached to $1,942.50 in escrow. With semantic tones the money-bearing threads separate at a glance. Refs: Intercom (conversation list carries state), Copilot. (S1)

    CHANGE Two columns on desktop. A separate /messages/[id] route per reply means a navigation for each of five conversations. The list-plus-thread pattern is universal here for a reason. The route stays for deep links and for mobile. Refs: Intercom, Twist, Copilot, Cloaked.
  3. 3
    ADD A contract strip above the thread, with a route into the room. The contract room already embeds chat; the inbox is the same conversation seen from the other direction and currently carries none of the contract. One strip, plus "Open the purchase", closes the loop.

What changes

  1. 1
    ADD Live availability on the item strip. Enquiry threads outlive listings — the most common marketplace message is "is this still available?" and the answer is already in the row. Showing "sold 3 Sep" saves the exchange entirely.
  2. 2

What changes

  1. 1
    ADD A "needs you before a deadline" block, pinned above everything. The five current types classify notifications by subject — offer, message, trade, sale, system — not by whether anything happens if they are ignored. On a platform where inspection windows auto-complete, hold authorisations lapse and return deadlines expire, that is the wrong axis. The consequence sentence is the point: "do nothing and $1,757.50 is paid to them" is information a member cannot currently get from a notification.
  2. 2
    ADD Date grouping. Today / Earlier this week is how every reference inbox reads, and it is cheap. Ref: Cloaked groups by "Today".
  3. 3
    CHANGE Deadline items keep their action on mobile. A notification that says a deadline is passing and offers only a link to go and find it is a notification that gets dismissed.
  4. —
    KEEP Fire-and-forget email threaded per contract for the deadline, dispute and payout events — that pairing is already right, and this proposal makes the in-app view match the email's urgency.

What changes

  1. 1
    CHANGE Five sections instead of three tabs. The current verification tab holds two independent gates and the profile tab holds a saved card, which is neither a profile nor a verification. Splitting them makes each page answer one question. Refs: Plain, Melio — grouped settings navs.
  2. 2
    ADD Every field states who can see it. This is the most valuable change on the sheet. The platform holds a public alias and a provider-verified legal name, and the whole privacy model rests on members understanding which is which — but the form currently presents them as two text fields. Naming the audience per field also makes the "bio sits outside the verified block" decision visible rather than merely true. Ref: Plain — "Full name. Visible to your team members." / "Public name. Visible to customers."
  3. 3
    ADD Explicit Save and Discard. A form where some fields save on blur and some on submit is a form members do not trust. Ref: Melio — Cancel / Save at the foot of the form.
  4. 4
    ADD Surface a blocked payout at the top of the mobile account page. Money that cannot reach a member should be visible from the page they land on, not only from the payouts sub-page they have no reason to open.
  5. —
    KEEP The verified name as read-only and provider-sourced, and never accepting card details into any form on our side — the saved-card row is a Stripe element and a brand/last4 read back from Stripe, and nothing here changes that.

What changes

  1. 1
    CHANGE A blocked payout gets a banner that separates the money from the problem. SELLER_NOT_PAYABLE has real money behind it, and a seller's first reading of any payment warning is "my money is gone". Name the amount, say it is safe, say sales are unaffected, give one action. Ref: Whop — near-identical banner and wording pattern.
  2. 2
    ADD Four states, with the blocked one emphasised. Held / frozen / queued / paid are the four real places a seller's money can be, and the read model already computes them. "Held in escrow — not yours yet" is the honest label and it is better for the platform too: it sets the expectation that completion, not sale, is when money is earned. Refs: Upwork four-metric row; Stripe Balances — each figure with a sentence explaining it.
  3. 3
    ADD Gross, fee and net as columns. The 5% fee is charged per contract and a seller currently has no single place that reconciles what a card sold for against what arrived. This is also the page an accountant asks for, hence the CSV export. Refs: Upwork transactions table; Dub.
  4. 4
    ADD Say we are not a bank. Escrowed funds are commingled in the platform balance — that is inherent to hold-then-release on Connect, not a shortcut — and a page showing four money figures under a seller's name should not read as an account statement. Ref: Whop carries the equivalent disclosure under its balance table.
  5. 5
    CHANGE Mobile leads with the blocker, then the four figures at half size. Same information, reordered by urgency.
  6. —
    KEEP Excluding trade collateral from these figures — an uncaptured authorisation is not money the platform holds, and counting it would invent a balance that does not exist.
