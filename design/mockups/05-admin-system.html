What changes

  1. 1
    CHANGE Custody reconciliation goes to the top and gets a named "unexplained difference". Every other figure on this console is derived from our own rows, so it can only ever agree with itself. This one compares the provider's balance against what we believe we owe, which makes it the single control that can catch a chargeback, a provider fee, or an automatic sweep — and none of those write a row anywhere. Naming the delta and stating what a non-zero value means turns a reporting panel into an alarm.
  2. 2
    CHANGE Group by "money that hasn't moved" rather than by feature. Stuck payouts, stale COLLATERAL_PENDING trades and holds about to lapse are four different tables today and one question operationally: what is at risk right now? The expiring hold in particular has a hard clock — an authorisation that lapses removes a trader's protection silently.
  3. 3
    ADD Mobile admin is read-only, and says so. Retrying a payout, resolving a fraud finding and capturing collateral are irreversible and move real money. A phone-sized target for any of them is a liability, and a staff member who knows the constraint up front stops looking for the button.
  4. —
    KEEP requireStaff() and requireAdmin() as two capabilities rather than a hierarchy — and showing the viewer which they hold, since nothing derives one from the other.

What changes

  1. 1
    ADD Publish the priority model in the rail. The ordering rule is a genuinely considered piece of design — deadline, then fraud, then SLA, and explicitly never amount — and a staff member who cannot see it will assume the queue is wrong when a $86 case outranks a $1,942 one.
  2. 2
    ADD A "why it's here" column. Each row states the fact that put it at its position, with the hard deadline first. "Hold lapses in 19h — capture becomes impossible after that" is the most operationally important sentence the queue can carry: after that moment a fraud finding cannot be paid, whatever the decision.
  3. 3
    KEEP ADD Read the contract, not the listing — and say so. Contract line items and the trade's goods description exist precisely so arbitration has something to adjudicate. Labelling the panel "as agreed" makes the guarantee explicit for the person relying on it.
  4. 4
    ADD Each outcome states its consequence, in money and in effect. A fraud finding is the most severe action in the product — a permanent ban plus an identity blocklist — and it currently looks like the third button in a list. Showing the amount and the effect per option is the difference between a decision and a click.
  5. —
    KEEP No identity dossier. The behavioural record is the right evidence surface, and the reason the Police Evidence Pack was withdrawn should stay visible on this page so nobody proposes it again.

What changes

  1. 1
    CHANGE Organise help by the questions the product raises, with stable anchors. The contract room's pay dialog already deep-links to /help#holds, which is the right instinct — the help page should be built around those destinations rather than as prose that happens to contain them.
  2. 2
    ADD Reuse the step timeline to explain escrow. The same component that shows a member where their own purchase is up to, used with no live data, is the clearest possible explanation — and it means the help page and the room teach the same shape.
  3. 3
    ADD Say the policies are not counsel-reviewed, and lead with a plain-terms summary. The product documentation already records that terms and privacy are product policy rather than legal advice. Members deserve the same disclosure, and the five bullets that matter — we hold money, we are not a bank, sellers are individuals, fraud means a ban, Australia only — are worth more than the seven sections beneath them.

What changes

  1. 1
    CHANGE A 404 on a listing should offer the two things the member wanted. "Sold or removed, and your account is fine" plus routes to similar cards and their watchlist. The distinction between "gone" and "broken" is already enforced server-side; the copy should carry it too.
  2. 2
    ADD An error reference, and a sentence about money. On a platform holding escrowed funds, the first fear behind any error page is financial. Say contracts and money are unaffected, and give support something to search for.
  3. 3
    ADD The suspension page needs an appeal route and a data export. This is the most consequential page in the product — a permanent ban following a staff fraud finding, with an identity blocklist behind it — and a dead end with no review path is both a fairness problem and a support problem, since the member will simply email from a new address. Saying what happened to their in-flight contracts closes the other obvious question.
  4. 4
    KEEP Skeletons that redraw the real chrome. Drawing the actual nav so the rail doesn't flash, and reserving heights from the type scale, is careful work and it should be the standard for any new loading state.
  5. 5
    CHANGE Every empty state says what would fill it. Most already do. The region-empty case is worth singling out: "trading is Australia-only for now" is a real, explicable limit, and a member who is told why does not conclude the app is broken.
