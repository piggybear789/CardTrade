The palette question — and a correction

Round 1 said the accent colours were over-saturated against a desaturated violet. That was wrong, and reading app/globals.css properly is what shows it: HSL saturation is not perceptual chroma. --trust at 80% 26% is a dark, muted teal, not a loud one, and the token comments record that six teal alternatives were measured — 173° won on contrast (5.26:1) and on hue distance from the violet (77°). --action had already been moved off safety orange (32° → 40°) and gained --action-border because a pastel fill cannot be its own edge. This palette is argued, not accidental.

What actually changes

  1. P1
    CHANGE One token: --destructive 0° → 352°. The two closest hues in the whole palette are --action (40°) and --destructive (0°) — 40° apart — and they carry the most opposed meanings in the product: "this is your move" and "this went wrong". Every other pair sits 77–133° apart. Moving red to 352° opens the gap to 48° and pulls it into the 275°-tinted family the neutrals already live in. Luminance is unchanged at constant S/L, so the measured 6.46:1 and 6.67:1 still hold. --action deliberately does not move — its 40° is a reference to the wordmark orange, per the token's own comment.
  2. P2
    CHANGE Take violet off prices. This is the real answer to "don't abuse the purple". The problem was never the hue, it was the frequency: --iris-ink is on every price in the app, which is the single most repeated element there is, so violet ended up meaning "money" as well as "platform". Prices become ink. Violet then means exactly one thing — the platform's own presence: the rail, the active nav item, custody of funds, the focus ring. It also settles the desktop/mobile disagreement on the listing route in favour of the readable one.
  3. P3
    ADD Five status tones out of the four existing hues. No new token. The reason six cash-sale statuses collapse onto one solid violet chip is that violet is doing both "brand" and "in progress". Splitting on fill-versus-outline within one hue separates them honestly — both are the platform holding something, differing in intensity — and no fifth hue has to be invented to fit into a wheel that has no room for one.
    Agreeing terms neutral — nothing committed yet. Also negotiating, cancelled.
    ● In transit in progress — iris outline. Posted, in transit, negotiating.
    ● Funds held money is held — iris filled. Funds confirmed, inspecting.
    Your move waiting on this member — action amber.
    ✓ Completed settled — trust teal. Completed, paid, verified.
    ● Disputed halted — destructive. Disputed, failed, return required.

Defects found in the current build

These are not design preferences. Each was found by reading the source and each is a thing the code does that it almost certainly does not intend to do. They are ordered by what they cost a member.

Bugs

  1. B1
    Step labels describe the viewer in the third person — and they are the largest text in the action card. deriveCashSaleSteps emits label: 'Buyer confirms the item arrived' and 'Seller ships with tracking', and that label is the dock's <h3>. So the buyer reads "Buyer confirms the item arrived" about themselves. The detail beneath it is correctly second-person ("Confirm receipt, or report it as not received") — but that line is hidden below md, so on a phone the only copy shown is the one in the wrong voice. Voice is also inconsistent between steps: "Set handover terms" is imperative, "Payment collected and held" is passive, "Buyer confirms…" is third person. domain/contract/cashSaleSteps.ts · components/contract/ContractActionCard.tsx
  2. B2
    InspectionCountdown renders in two different places in two rooms, and in the cash sale it is behind a sheet. The component is good and its copy is exactly what the review asked for — "Inspection window · 2 days left", then "accept it or raise a dispute by {date}. {expiryConsequence}". In the trade room it is a full-width banner above the panes. In the cash sale it is inside the Protection tab, which on a phone is behind the bottom sheet — so the deadline that silently completes the sale and pays the seller is two taps from being seen. Same component, one visible and one not. CashSaleView.tsx Protection tab vs TradeContract.tsx banner
  3. B3
    The onboarding step counter never says "1". PROGRESS_STEPS = STEPS = ['welcome','username','region','intent'], so the count is out of 4 — but the dot row is hidden on welcome. The first number a member ever sees is therefore "Step 2 of 4", and the last is "4 of 4" on a screen that is followed by more work for sellers.

    NOT A BUG and worth recording as such, because round 1 called it one: seller-onboarding is excluded from STEPS deliberately, and the comment above the array gives the reason — it "sits off the end for sellers only, and counting it would tell a buyer they are on step 4 of 5 of something they will never see." That reasoning is correct. The fix is not to fold it in; it is to stop counting welcome, so the visible sequence reads 1–2–3 of 3, and to give the seller step its own two-item progress for identity and payouts. components/onboarding/OnboardingWizard.tsx
  4. B4
    CANCELLED renders as a different badge variant depending on which room you are in — outline on a cash sale, secondary on a trade. One state, two appearances, for no stated reason. CashSaleStatusBadge.tsx vs StateBadge.tsx
  5. B5
    Two search fields on desktop with different scopes and identical appearance. CatalogFilterSearch in the rail filters only the items already loaded; HeaderSearch in the dark bar runs the real query. Both are a magnifier and a text box, so on page 1 of 27 the rail field silently searches 48 of 1,284 items. Not being actioned this round — recorded because it will keep being reported. components/listings/CatalogControls.tsx
  6. B6
    The same fact is named two different things one click apart. The profile tab is "Payout setup"; the card inside it is headed "Payout destination". The card's comment explains at length why it must never say "Verified Account" — that reasoning is right and the naming is still inconsistent with the tab that contains it. components/profile/PayoutOnboarding.tsx
  7. B7
    The action button is 1.55:1 against the page and depends entirely on its border. This is documented rather than accidental — --action-border exists for it — but it means any usage of --action that drops the border silently degrades the primary buy control into a tag. Worth stating as a rule rather than a comment on a token. app/globals.css --action / --action-border
